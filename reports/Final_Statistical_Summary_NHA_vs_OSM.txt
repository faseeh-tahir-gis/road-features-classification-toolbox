13. METHODOLOGICAL NOTES AND LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section documents known methodological limitations and open items in this report, so results are read with appropriate caution and can be improved in a follow-up pass. It does not change any figure reported above.

a) Reference vs. truth: NHA is used here as the AUTHORITATIVE reference dataset for classification (Final_Class = NHA_Class), but this is a methodological choice, not a claim that NHA is error-free. All figures above measure OSM's agreement WITH NHA, not absolute correctness of either dataset.
b) Name/route-code matching is supporting evidence only (Section A.3.1 in the tool configuration) and can upgrade a spatial match's status, but a name/ref match is never by itself proof that two features represent the same physical road, and a name mismatch does not by itself prove they do not.
c) The Perfect Match definition (Section 5) currently combines classification agreement and geometric alignment tier only. It does not separately require positional deviation below a fixed absolute threshold, confirmed route-code identity, or absence of a Match_Count > 1 ambiguity; a stricter definition folding in those factors would very likely produce a lower Perfect Match count than reported.
d) The 5 / 15 / 50 m alignment tolerances and the 10 m positional tolerance are configured constants (ALIGNMENT_PERFECT_TOLERANCE etc.) chosen as reasonable defaults for this comparison; they are not yet derived from documented NHA/OSM positional accuracy specifications or source mapping scale, and should be justified or re-derived from that source metadata before the tiers are used for formal acceptance/rejection decisions.
e) The Motorway/National-Highway OSM classification crosswalk (NHA_OSM_AGREEMENT) - i.e. that OSM Trunk or Primary is an acceptable representation of an NHA National Highway - reflects general OSM tagging practice in Pakistan (motorways are consistently tagged 'motorway'; national highways are inconsistently split across 'trunk'/'primary') but is an editable assumption (Section A.3 of the tool) and should be stated as such wherever this report's classification figures are cited.
f) This report is one-directional (NHA -> OSM). It answers 'how much of the NHA reference network is represented in OSM' (Section 8) but cannot compute how many OSM Motorway/Trunk/Primary features have NO NHA counterpart, or how many are duplicate OSM representations of the same road, because X05_Final_Road_Classification is one row per NHA feature. A complementary OSM-to-NHA pass is needed for a full bidirectional completeness picture.
g) The OSM comparison universe is the pre-filtered working copy (motorway/motorway_link/trunk/trunk_link/primary/primary_link only, 6899 of the full OSM road dataset), so all OSM-side figures in this report describe agreement within that subset, not the complete OSM road network. Link-road subtypes (motorway_link, trunk_link, primary_link) are currently folded into their parent class (Motorway/Trunk/Primary respectively) rather than tracked as a separate category.
h) No visual/manual spot-check of matched, mismatched, and unmatched features has been performed as part of this automated run; automated spatial/attribute matching can produce false matches and false non-matches, so a sample review in ArcMap is recommended before the figures above are treated as final.
i) All statistics above are network-wide totals/percentages and do not break results out by individual route (e.g. per motorway or per national highway) or by geographic area. A route-level and/or map-based breakdown would show whether mismatches are spread evenly or concentrated in specific corridors or regions.
j) NHA and OSM may have been captured or last edited at different times; this report does not record or account for the acquisition/edit dates of either input layer, so some discrepancies above may reflect legitimate temporal change (e.g. a newly built or realigned road) rather than a data or matching error.
k) Feature counts and length-based measures are reported separately throughout (e.g. Sections 1, 8 vs 9, 10) deliberately, since OSM's segmentation means a single NHA road can correspond to a large or small number of OSM features independent of how much of its length is actually represented; the two measures should continue to be read side by side, not interchangeably.
